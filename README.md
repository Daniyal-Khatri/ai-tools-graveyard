# AI Tools Graveyard 🪦

> Every AI tool dies one day. This is where we remember them — and point you to what is still alive.

![graves](https://img.shields.io/badge/graves-55-4c566a) ![buried](https://img.shields.io/badge/buried-%244.24B-9e2b25) ![license](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-MIT-blue)

A hand-checked list of **55 AI tools that shut down, got bought and killed, or were discontinued** — each one with the date it died, the cause of death, a source that proves it, and the free alternative that survived it.

**Money buried here so far: $4.24 billion** — the total raised by the 36 funded startups on this list. The rest were internal big-company products or self-funded, so no public number exists.

Made by [SaaSListy](https://saaslisty.com) — a directory of AI tools and the workflows they belong to. We check tools by hand for a living, so we notice when one dies.

## The Rules

1. **No source, no tombstone.** Every death on this list is backed by the company's own announcement or real reporting. The tool's name links to the proof.
2. **Rumors don't count.** If a tool is still running, it is not here — even if it is in trouble. Dying tools go in Intensive Care below, not in the ground.
3. **Every grave points somewhere alive.** Each entry names a free or freemium alternative that still works today, so you always have a next step.

## The Biggest Graves

- **[Sora](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320557/20260715/pixverse-closes-439m-round-targets-ai-game-engines-after-sora-exits-market.htm)** (died April 26, 2026) — OpenAI's video app — reportedly burning around $1M a day, gone less than a year after launch.
- **[Builder.ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Builder.ai)** (died May 20, 2025) — Raised $450M+ promising apps 'like ordering pizza', then collapsed into insolvency in 2025.
- **[Humane AI Pin](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead-as-hp-buys-startups-assets-for-116m/)** (died February 28, 2025) — The $699 pin that was going to replace your phone. HP bought the pieces; every pin stopped working on Feb 28, 2025.
- **[Moxie](https://killedbyai.net/)** (died December 9, 2024) — A $799 companion robot for kids. The company died, the robots stopped talking, and there were no refunds.
- **[PlayHT](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/top-playht-alternatives)** (died December 31, 2025) — Meta hired the whole team and killed the product — every user's voice clones were deleted with no export.

## What the Graves Tell Us

Patterns from the data above — every number here is counted from the dataset, not guessed:

- **The die-off is accelerating.** Deaths by year: 2022: 1 · 2023: 6 · 2024: 12 · 2025: 18 · 2026: 18. 36 of the 55 tools here died in 2025–2026 alone.
- **AI tools die young.** The average lifespan on this list is **4.9 years**. 21 of 55 were dead within 3 years; only 5 made it past 10.
- **How they died:** 31 shut down, 16 were bought and killed, 8 were dropped when the company pivoted.
- **The team-hire is a death sentence.** In at least 5 cases here — PlayHT (Meta), Humanloop (Anthropic), Supermaven (Cursor), Relay.app (Google), Hour One (Wix) — a big company took the team or the tech and the product was shut down soon after. Not once did the product survive it.
- **Money doesn't save you.** The typical funded startup here raised about **$22 million** and still died in under five years. The biggest grave, Olive, raised $902 million and was sold off in pieces.

## ⚠️ Warning Signs Your AI Tool Is About to Die

Every sign below already happened to at least one tool in this graveyard. None of them guarantee a death — but when you see one, export your data the same week.

1. **A big company hires the team.** PlayHT, Humanloop, Supermaven, Relay.app, Hour One — the product shut down soon after in every case here. The users were never part of the deal.
2. **The owner ships its own replacement.** Cortana died when Copilot arrived. Google Assistant died when Gemini took over. When the successor launches, the old tool's days are counted.
3. **An "exciting new chapter" acquisition post.** Nine tools here were bought and later killed. The announcement always says nothing will change for users. Then, months later, the shutdown email arrives.
4. **It's free, popular, and expensive to run.** Figgs AI passed a million users and still closed because the free service cost too much to run. Yupp closed with 1.3M users despite raising $33M. Popular is not the same as profitable.
5. **There is no export button.** PlayHT deleted every user's voice clones with no way to download them. Microsoft deleted every GPT people built in Copilot GPT Builder. If your work can't leave, your work dies with the tool.
6. **The hardware needs their servers.** The Humane AI Pin became a brick the day the service closed. Moxie robots stopped talking to the kids who owned them. Servers off = device dead.
7. **The updates quietly stop.** No changelog, no new features, support answers get slower — then the shutdown post. Phind closed abruptly and user data was deleted weeks later. Silence is a symptom, not a coincidence.
